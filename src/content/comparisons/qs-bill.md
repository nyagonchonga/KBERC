# The Quantity Surveyors Bill vs 2026 KBERC Draft

## 1. Deep Dive: Controlling the Money & The Hybrid Rejection

### The QS Push for Independence
The **Quantity Surveyors Bill** was the mirror to the Architects Bill. Following decades of dissatisfaction with the joint BORAQS board (Cap 525), the Institute of Quantity Surveyors of Kenya (IQSK) pushed heavily for their own standalone regulatory body. 

Their argument was fundamentally economic: Quantity Surveyors manage the money. They control the Bills of Quantities (BQs), the valuations, and the final project costings. The IQSK argued that regulating the financial mechanics of construction requires a vastly different skill set than regulating architectural design, and therefore warranted an exclusive, autonomous QS Board.

### The Asymmetric Hybrid Model: Why QS Was Directly Incorporated
The **2026 KBERC Draft** brutally rejects the standalone QS Bill. 

Under the State's **"Asymmetric Hybrid Model"**, Engineers were allowed to keep their autonomous board (EBK), but Architects and Quantity Surveyors were transitioned into a direct incorporation structure under KBERC as their "Primary Regulator."

The Quantity Surveyors point to this as massive political hypocrisy: *"If the Engineers are a highly functional 'plug-in' to KBERC, why couldn't the proposed QS Board also be a plug-in? We manage the money; we don't design the buildings. We deserve autonomy."*

### 2026 Motivation (The State's Financial Grip)
The State’s refusal to grant QS autonomy stems from a single terrifying reality: **Corruption.**
The State views the costing of mega-projects (dams, highways, government housing) as the primary vector for public sector graft. Bloated BQs, massive variations, and rigged valuations cost the taxpayer billions. 

The State argues it cannot afford to let QS self-regulate through an autonomous board. By assimilating QS directly into KBERC, the State (which holds significant voting power on the KBERC Council) gains direct, immediate oversight over the profession that controls the money. KBERC isn't just about structural safety; it is an anti-corruption mechanism designed to force radical transparency in construction costing.

## 2. Exhaustive Pros & Cons

### The Standalone Quantity Surveyors Bill
**Advantages:**
- **Financial Specialization:** Allows the board to focus purely on the mathematics of construction, dispute resolution, and contractual law, free from design interference.
- **Accurate Cost Indices:** A dedicated QS board could more effectively publish mandatory, real-time national cost indices for materials, stabilizing the chaotic construction market.
- **Peer-to-Peer Dispute Resolution:** Financial disputes between contractors and clients would be arbitrated by veteran QS professionals who intimately understand complex contract clauses.

**Disadvantages:**
- **The Loophole for Graft:** A board composed entirely of QS professionals (industry capture) might be too lenient in punishing peers who inflate government contracts or approve fraudulent variations.
- **The Communication Breakdown:** Removing QS from the Architects creates a silo where the designer and the cost-manager answer to different authorities, often leading to unbuildable, over-budget designs.

### 2026 KBERC Draft (Direct Incorporation)
**Advantages:**
- **Forced Financial Transparency:** By assimilating QS into a multi-disciplinary board heavily watched by State appointees, it becomes exponentially harder to pass bloated BQs without scrutiny.
- **The Unified Tribunal:** Financial disputes are escalated to the neutral Built Environment Appeals Tribunal, ensuring legal experts—not just industry peers—judge contractual fraud.

**Disadvantages:**
- **Loss of Autonomy:** QS professionals lose their dream of absolute independence. They must share power on the KBERC Council with Architects, whose primary goal is design, not budget.
- **The Engineering Resentment:** QS professionals are deeply frustrated that they must submit to KBERC's direct rule while Engineers (EBK) remain an autonomous, federated sub-agency.

## 3. Deep Clause Breakdown

### Part I: Definitions & The Scale of Fees
- **QS Bill:** Heavily protects the strict, mandatory Scale of Fees that QS charge clients, ensuring their income is legally guaranteed based on a percentage of the project cost.
- **2026 Draft:** KBERC takes over the power to publish binding Minimum Scales of Fees. However, because KBERC regulates multiple professions, the State can harmonize the fees across Architects and QS simultaneously, preventing one profession from gouging the client.

### Part II: Administration & Board Composition
- **QS Bill:** The Board is completely dominated by IQSK nominees. It is the ultimate form of self-regulation.
- **2026 Draft:** Because KBERC is the direct Primary Regulator for QS, the voting power is balanced. QS must share the Council evenly with Architects, State Appointees, and consumer representatives, replacing IQSK's exclusive control with a collaborative model.

### Part III: Education & Accreditation
- **QS Bill:** The QS Board wields supreme statutory power to accredit university programs focused on construction economics and project management.
- **2026 Draft:** QS lose this supreme power. While KBERC creates a Quantity Surveying Technical Committee to advise on curriculum, the final statutory hammer belongs to the multi-disciplinary KBERC Council. This means Architects and State officials have a vote on what constitutes proper QS education.

### Part IV: Financial Liability & Discipline (The 20 Million Shilling Hammer)
- **QS Bill:** Relies on internal peer review to handle professional misconduct. Fines for malpractice are relatively standard.
- **2026 Draft:** KBERC views QS fraud as a massive public interest issue. Misconduct is routed directly to the neutral Built Environment Appeals Tribunal. Furthermore, KBERC introduces massive **Corporate Professional Indemnity Insurance**. If a QS firm is found guilty of gross negligence or fraudulent valuation that collapses a project's financing, the fines and insurance payouts are designed to be financially ruinous (upwards of Sh20,000,000), acting as a massive deterrent against graft.
